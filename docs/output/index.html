Output — displays, fullscreen, the operator window, Resolume

1. One display or two

Plugin Player always opens as one window on your main display, whatever is plugged in. The projector output is something you turn on, once:

  1. Plug the projector in (Windows: Win+P → Extend).
  2. Click the orange ⧉ OUTPUT button next to the PERFORM / MAP / CONTROLS tabs (Output to your TV screen, monitor, projector, or LED wall). A box opens: click OUTPUT TO SCREEN 2. Nothing goes out until you choose. With two or more, a picker opens (see Two, three or more screens below). With none, a panel says what to do (plug one in over HDMI / USB-C, Windows + P › Extend, click OUTPUT again) and offers FULL SCREEN ON THIS SCREEN or a test window. The same switch is in the header's OUT chip: ON SCREEN 2 ▸ (or OFF ▸) → OUTPUT ON SCREEN 2 ▸.
  3. The projector gets the picture fullscreen, at once; your window stays exactly as it was (§3): nothing restarts, the scene, the look, the camera and your panels stay put, and the show never stops. Turning it off just takes the picture off the projector the same way.

Plugin Player remembers your pick on this computer: next time the projector is plugged in, the output opens on it by itself (party use stays plug-and-go). While it is on, the button reads OUTPUT ON · SCREEN 2; click it (or OUT ▸) for OUTPUT OFF · ONE WINDOW, another screen, or MAP IT ONTO THE WALL. Turning it off is remembered too. A build that sends to Resolume over Spout has no ⧉ OUTPUT button (Resolume owns the screens there; OUT ▸ still works). Closing the app (the X, Alt+F4) always closes the projector picture with it.

What resolution reaches the projector. Plugin Player always renders at 1920×1080, whatever the size of the app window, the screen it's on or the projector. Drag the window small, turn OUTPUT on or off, add a second projector: the picture doesn't change. Every screen OUTPUT is on gets the same 1920×1080. A projector that isn't 16:9 (a 1280×800, say) shows the whole picture with black bars, never stretched; a 4K screen shows the 1080p picture scaled up. Scenes you own and the free scenes go out at 1080p with no watermark. A scene you don't own yet goes out at 720p detail with the watermark, and the moment you buy it, it's 1080p and clean: no restart, no black frame. Spout and REC take the same 1920×1080. 4K output is planned for after the beta.

The OUT 1920×1080 chip in the header is the render size (--size WxH changes it, for Spout and REC too; --spout-size sets Spout alone), then OUT ▸ (the projector output, above), then MAP. On a narrow screen the OUT chip folds into the ⋯ menu right after ⧉ OUTPUT; it works the same there, and the SHOW CHECKLIST's OUTPUT ON SCREEN 2 ▸ button opens it too.

Windows must be in Extend mode (Win+P → Extend). In Duplicate mode there is only one display and the laptop screen shows the same picture.

Two, three or more screens

⧉ OUTPUT never guesses: a click opens the screen picker, even with one extra screen. The button shows a small 2 SCREENS / 3 SCREENS badge so you can see at a glance that more than one is connected.

  • A map of your screens at the top, arranged the way Windows (or macOS) has them, with your laptop marked THIS SCREEN. Click a box on the map or a card below it.
  • One card per screen: its number, its real name when the screen reports one (for example DELL U2720Q or EPSON PROJECTOR, otherwise SCREEN 3), its resolution and refresh rate (3840×2160 · 30 Hz), and which side of the laptop it sits on (← ON THE LEFT, ON THE RIGHT →, ↑ ABOVE). A screen showing the picture has a ✓ ON.
  • Output is off: one click on a card puts the picture full screen on that screen.
  • Output is already on and you click another screen: the picker asks you, right in the panel (never a pop-up): ADD SCREEN 3 · SHOW ON BOTH, which sends the picture to both screens, or MOVE TO SCREEN 3 · ONLY, which makes screen 3 the only output. CANCEL leaves things as they are.
  • Each screen that is on has its own TURN OFF. ALL OUTPUTS OFF · ONE WINDOW turns every screen off at once.
  • IDENTIFY SCREENS shows a big number and the screen's name on every extra screen for 3 seconds, so you can tell the projector from the TV. It never takes the keyboard or the controller away from your window.

Every screen shows the same picture with the same MAP (corners, soft edges, slices). Each extra screen costs about 0.04 ms of CPU per frame (one GPU copy of the program), so two or three screens play just as smoothly as one. A separate map for each screen isn't available yet (see below).

Plugging and unplugging during a show. The list updates as soon as a screen is plugged in (the button shows +1 SCREEN for a moment) or unplugged. If a screen showing the picture is unplugged (a kicked HDMI cable), that screen just goes off: the show keeps playing in your window and on any other screens that are on, and a message tells you what happened. Plug the same screen back in and the picture comes back on it by itself, even when Windows has given it a new number. The app never crashes and never leaves a full-screen window covering your laptop.

Remembered. Plugin Player remembers every screen you had on (the whole set). At the next launch it turns them all on again, finding each screen by its number and, if Windows has renumbered it, by its name and size.

A separate map per screen: not available yet. Right now the program is mapped once and every screen copies that mapped picture. A separate map for each screen means each mirror would draw the program through its own corner pins and soft edges: one mapping per screen saved in the show, a MAP screen picker, and a warp pass for each mirror (one extra GPU pass each) instead of a plain copy.

Already mapped in Resolume?

Drop your Arena Advanced Output preset (the .xml from Documents\Resolume Arena\Presets\Advanced Output) on Plugin Player and it uses the same slices: each slice takes the same part of the picture and lands in the same place on the projector, corner pins and bent edges included. Pick which screen when the preset has more than one. Plugin Player tells you in plain words what it could not take over (a disabled slice, a soft-edge blend, a colour trim, a polygon cut — polygons show as their rectangle for now). Starting from a terminal: --mapping "C:\…\Advanced Output\My Stage.xml", and --mapping-screen "Screen 2" to choose the screen. Edge blending and multi-projector stay in Resolume on purpose; Spout into Arena for those comes back after the beta (§4).

Output quality: 1080p or 720p

CONTROLS › OUTPUT QUALITY sets what Plugin Player renders at: 1080p (the default) or 720p. On a weaker computer 720p is the first thing to try: it's much less work for the graphics card, and most scenes run far smoother (a 2017 MacBook Pro goes from 7-32 fps at 1080p to 15-60 at 720p). The projector, Spout, Syphon and REC all follow it. It's remembered, and you can change it during a show: no restart, the picture dissolves across in under a second, and a scene with trails or fluid keeps them. Resolume sees the sender change size. The only time it waits is during a recording, because a recording keeps one size: stop REC first. A scene you own stays clean at either setting. A shortcut can set it too: --quality 720p.

2. Fullscreen output

  • G = chrome off. The grid, the panels and the header disappear and the picture fills the window. Keyboard and controller keep working. G again brings the chrome back.
  • F = fullscreen canvas (browser style). Esc or F leaves it. If you see fullscreen refused in the log, click once inside the window and press F again.
  • H hides the HUD text.

The footer line while the chrome is on reads like 300 FPS · 1920×1080 · PLUGIN PLAYER · V0.1.8-beta · WIN. The fps number is capped by your display's refresh rate when the chrome is off and fullscreen.

3. The operator window

With a projector plugged in (Extend), the laptop window is the operator window (§1): everything you click, the controller plate and the full-size live program monitor, while the projector window carries only the picture. The projector never shows the buttons. The show runs in the laptop window and the projector shows a copy of the same picture, frame for frame, so switching the output on, off or to another screen never restarts anything (it takes a few hundredths of a second). Minimizing the laptop window does not stop the projector. Plug the projector in, then OUT ▸ OUTPUT ON SCREEN 2: no restart needed. With two projectors the menu lists SCREEN 2 and SCREEN 3; picking the other one moves the output there.

4. Spout into Resolume

Spout is built into Plugin Player: nothing extra to install (no Python, no helper program). It works both ways: Plugin Player's picture into Resolume Arena (or any Spout app), and Arena's composition into Plugin Player (below).

  1. Start Plugin Player with --spout (see Where to type flags below). The SPOUT chip reads SPOUT ON.
  2. In Arena: Sources › Spout › Plugin Player → drag it into an empty clip. Change scenes on the pad: Arena follows in the same frame.
  3. Picture upside down in Arena? Restart with --spout-invert.

The sender is called Plugin Player (--spout-name "My name" to change it). It sends 1920×1080 at 60 frames a second by default (--spout-size, --spout-fps, never above 60). --spout --no-window renders with nothing on screen, when Arena owns the projector.

Match Plugin Player's frame rate to Resolume's

Plugin Player draws at most 60 frames a second (it used to draw as fast as the monitor goes: 144, 240 or more on a gaming monitor, all of it on the graphics card Resolume needs). Spout sends no more than that either.

  • If your Resolume is capped at 50 (its frame rate setting in Arena's Preferences), set Plugin Player to 50 too: CONTROLS › ADVANCED › MAX FRAME RATE › 50. It is remembered. Or put --max-fps 50 in the shortcut.
  • The choices are 30 · 50 · 60 · 120 · DISPLAY (DISPLAY = every frame the monitor shows, the old way; the most GPU).
  • Lower is lighter: 30 halves the graphics work of 60 and still looks smooth for slow fire.
  • --spout-fps still works (never above 60, never above the frame cap).

Plugin Player and Resolume on the same night

  • Map in one place only. Either Plugin Player's MAP tab sends the mapped picture straight to the projector (Resolume not involved), or Arena's Advanced Output maps a flat picture it gets from Plugin Player over Spout. Never both: a mapped picture mapped again comes out warped twice.
  • The webcam belongs to Plugin Player. Windows lets one app use a camera at a time, and the Effect Layer needs it. Resolume gets the picture with you in it through Spout; do not add the webcam as a source in Arena.
  • Spout follows you. Change scene, preset or Effect Layer on the pad and Arena gets the change in the same frame. Plugin Player draws each frame once and only copies it out for Spout.
  • Map in Arena, not twice. Spout carries the picture before Plugin Player's MAP-tab mapping, even when a mapping is on. Plugin Player's own window still shows its mapping.
  • Both apps share the graphics card. Keep the laptop on its charger, set Windows power mode to Best performance, put both apps on the high-performance graphics card (NVIDIA Control Panel), keep Arena's composition at 1920×1080, and match the frame rate to Arena's (above). Resolume drops frames as soon as Plugin Player opens? TROUBLESHOOTING.md › Resolume drops frames when Plugin Player is open.
  • The CHECK panel's SPOUT row tells you whether the sender is up.

Use Resolume (or any Spout app) as the input picture

The other direction: Arena's composition comes INTO Plugin Player and becomes the picture every scene plays with, exactly like a camera. Built in, like Spout out (nothing to install):

  1. In Arena, send the composition out over Spout: Output › Advanced… → pick a screen → Device: Spout (a virtual output). Arena's Output menu also has a Composition output sharing section that can share the whole composition.
  2. In Plugin Player, click ▾ on the CAM chip. Under SPOUT you see every Spout sender that is sending right now, tagged SPOUT, with its size and frame rate (1920×1080 · 60 fps). The list updates by itself every few seconds.
  3. Click Arena's sender. It goes on the scene like a camera (ON SCENE): − / + take it off / put it back, CAMERA OFF SHOWS still decides what plays while it is off, REC records it, MEASURE DELAY works on it (below).
  4. Plugin Player remembers it by name. If Arena is not sending at the next launch, Plugin Player uses your camera and says so; pick Arena again under SPOUT when it sends.

Good to know:

  • Arena closed or its Spout output turned off mid-show: the chip reads LOST, the scenes play the demo clip, and the Spout sender stays in the list as GONE · WAITING. It comes back by itself (and back on the scene) the moment Arena sends again.
  • Plugin Player's own Spout output is never in the list: picking it would only loop straight back.
  • Back and forth (Plugin Player → Arena → Plugin Player) is allowed. When Arena's composition contains Plugin Player's own picture, what you send comes back a few frames later: video feedback (trails, tunnels). It is an effect, not a bug, and the panel says so in one line: FEEDBACK LOOP. To stop it, pick another sender or take Plugin Player out of the Arena composition.
  • MEASURE DELAY on a Spout input flashes the picture you SEND (it goes out over Spout, through Arena, and back in), so the number is the whole round trip. It needs Spout out on and Plugin Player in the Arena composition, full screen.
  • The Effect Layer treats Arena's picture as a scene, not as you: on AUTO the fluid comes off the picture's motion. Pick YOU by hand if Arena is sending a camera of a person.
  • The picture is scaled to the same feed as a camera (at most 1280×720) before the scenes see it, so a 4K composition costs no more than a 1080p one inside the scenes.

On a Mac: Syphon

On a Mac the same thing is Syphon (the Mac's Spout), built in the same way: nothing to install.

  • Start Plugin Player with --spout (or --share, the same flag on both platforms). The chip reads SYPHON ON.
  • In Arena on the Mac: Sources › Syphon Servers › Plugin Player → drag it into an empty clip. (--spout-name "My name" makes it Plugin Player - My name.)
  • Into Plugin Player: in Arena turn on Output › Composition output sharing → Texture sharing (Syphon); it shows up under CAM ▾ › SYPHON as Arena - Composition. Everything above (ON SCENE, LOST, FEEDBACK LOOP, MEASURE DELAY) works the same.
  • Needs macOS 13 (Ventura) or newer.
  • Both apps share one graphics chip: on a small MacBook Air keep the output at 1280×720 (--size 1280x720) and expect fewer frames a second than on a Mac with more graphics power.

Where to type flags

Run Plugin Player from a terminal: "Plugin Player.exe" --spout [to confirm at beta: the installed executable name and whether the shortcut accepts flags in its Target field].
