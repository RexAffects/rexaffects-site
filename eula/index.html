6. Flashing content and audience safety (please read)

6.1 Warning. Plugin Player produces fast-moving, high-contrast and flashing or strobing images, especially when you hit the beat buttons or use strobe parameters. A small number of people (roughly 1 in 4,000, more among children and teenagers) have photosensitive epilepsy and can have a seizure from flashing lights or patterns, even with no previous history. Symptoms include dizziness, altered vision, eye or face twitching, jerking limbs, disorientation, confusion or momentary loss of awareness. Stop using the app and seek medical advice if you or anyone watching has any of these. Don't use the Software if you have, or think you may have, photosensitive epilepsy, unless a doctor has said it's fine.

6.2 You are the operator. When you put the output on a projector, LED wall, stream or screen that other people can see, you are the one presenting it, not us. You are responsible for: warning your audience where flashing lights are used (some places require a posted warning, e.g. Maryland Health-General §24-206; many venues and event guides require signage at the entrance and on tickets); keeping flash rates within the limits that apply to you (widely used broadcast and accessibility guidance says no more than 3 flashes per second and strobes at or below 4 Hz, with limits on how much of the screen flashes and on saturated red; see ITU-R BT.1702, WCAG 2.3.1 and Ofcom's guidance); checking the venue's rules and licence conditions; and briefing venue staff. If you let a member of the public operate the controller ("guest mode"), the same applies to what they trigger.

6.3 What we do on our side. The app's own test flash (the camera latency test) is kept below 3 flashes per second, warns you first and has a dimmer option. We don't test every scene and every parameter against a flash analyser, and the speed of most flashes depends on how you play them. If you need a certified-safe output for broadcast, run it through a Harding or similar analyser yourself.
